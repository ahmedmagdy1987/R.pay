

########## JUDGE: BRAND-STRATEGIST ##########
# JUDGMENT — R.PAY CONCEPT REVIEW

## SCORES

---

### CONCEPT 1 — COMMAND / THE BRIDGE — **9/10**

**Strongest thing:** The law "**cyan means live**". It is the only art-direction rule in the six that converts the existing brand asset from decoration into *semantics*, and it is the mechanism by which this concept earns "premium" honestly rather than performing it. Everything downstream follows from one sentence: if it glows, it is reporting state. That is a system, not a mood. Its close second is **§03, الأسئلة الأربعة** — the operator's own 11pm questions, set as plain type with zero effects. That is the only section in all six documents that demonstrates the writer has met the customer, and it is where the deal actually closes.

**Near-fatal flaw:** It is the closest of the six to the site R.Pay already has. Near-black navy, cyan, mono telemetry, hairlines — a stranger comparing old and new sees discipline, not a leap, and the brief explicitly forbids evolving the existing site. Compounding it: "control room chic" is now the house style of every observability and infra company on earth, so its originality is borrowed from a *different* category's cliché rather than invented. It survives this because the metaphor is literally true of the product, but it wins on fit, not on freshness, and it must be pushed harder than written to escape the charge.

---

### CONCEPT 2 — NETWORK / SILENT GRID — **6/10**

**Strongest thing:** The **export card** (`احفظ اللقطة` → 1080×1350 → `navigator.share`). This is the only concept in the six that engineered for how this market actually spreads: a WhatsApp forward between two operators. Everyone else says "people will screenshot this." This one built the screenshot. Runner-up: reframing the cheap AI films inside a 320×180 **sensor-feed frame with a timecode and a LIVE dot** — a single move that upgrades a weak existing asset into an on-brand one at zero production cost.

**Fatal flaw:** It draws a map of Saudi Arabia and puts 97 dots on it. Ninety-seven. The concept names this risk itself and then fails to solve it, because it is not solvable — the moment you render the Kingdom you invite the viewer to measure your coverage against it, and the honest answer is three cities. This is a concept whose central image actively argues *against* the claim "أكبر مشغّل في المنطقة." The proposed mitigation (crop to the populated corridor, carry scale in the arcs) is an admission that the hero image has to be hidden from the viewer.

---

### CONCEPT 3 — TAP / UNBROKEN — **7/10**

**Strongest thing:** The information architecture *is* the product architecture. The critique of the features grid — that a grid of twelve boxes implicitly tells the buyer these are twelve unrelated things he must evaluate separately, when R.Pay's whole claim is that they are one chain — is the sharpest single paragraph of strategy in the entire submission set. And it ends with the causal line becoming the **left border of the CTA button**, which is the best craft detail in the six.

**Fatal flaw:** It sells the commodity half of the product. The chain it follows so beautifully — tap, auth, rail, settle — is the part SurePay and Geidea also do. The differentiated half (fleet command, 97 machines, the geofence, the prize ledger) arrives as stations 06–08, i.e. as *late chapters in someone else's film*. Secondary but real: it requires a two-day plate shoot it honestly admits to, it withholds the headline until scroll position 2 in a mobile market that bounces before scroll 2, and its own named failure mode — the procurement manager trapped inside an art film — is mitigated by inventing a nav and calling it a progress bar.

---

### CONCEPT 4 — RESTRAINT / DAYLIGHT — **8/10**

**Strongest thing:** The strategic observation is correct and nobody else made it: **the category is uniformly dark, therefore go white.** It is the only concept here that is identifiable with the logo cropped off, at WhatsApp thumbnail size — which is the real test of a brand in this market. And it makes restraint *argumentative* rather than tasteful: the competitor's site is dark because it is hiding a thin product; R.Pay's is lit because it has 465,255 transactions and Roshn's logo. Form and claim finally agree. The single dark section (غرفة التحكم) earning its darkness is the best structural move in the set — it makes the entire preceding white page retroactively make sense.

**Near-fatal flaw:** Its named failure mode is *"the client loses his nerve in week two."* This client has already rejected eight builds for lacking wow. Handing him a near-effectless white page and asking him to hold the line for five weeks against his own instincts is a bet on the client's temperament, not on the design — and it is a bet this concept loses, at which point cyan gets bolted onto white and the result is worse than either pure option. Underneath that sits a second problem: it depends on a licensed Arabic face and a person who can genuinely *set* Arabic. Neither is guaranteed, and with no effects to hide behind, there is no partial credit — it ships perfect or it ships broken.

---

### CONCEPT 5 — OBJECT / AXIS — **5/10**

**Strongest thing:** Naming the hardware. **محور ٠١ / AXIS 01** converts R.Pay from a service company with a website into a company that makes a thing, and that reframe is free, permanent, and worth more than the rest of the concept. The related insight — that the terminal on a phone screen is at roughly 1:1 physical scale, so mobile isn't a reduction but the actual object — is genuinely clever.

**Fatal flaw:** It is a mood board with a state machine attached, and it is selling something R.Pay probably doesn't own. The document confidently specifies `IP54`, `EMV L1/L2`, `112 mm`, a secure element, a six-layer exploded section — for a device this company almost certainly sources rather than manufactures. "This was designed, not sourced" is the beat it asks the section to deliver, and it may simply be a lie. Then it stakes everything on renders the brief already describes as glossy AI plastic, admits in its own risk section that it should not be built if those renders can't be funded, and buries the actual moat — the fleet platform — in section 07 as a reward for having loved the object first. The buyer does not lie awake thinking about the anodised finish on his card reader. Also: "black object on a plinth, lit like a Leica" is not an escape from cliché, it is the single most imitated page in technology.

---

### CONCEPT 6 — LEDGER / ONE RIYAL — **6.5/10**

**Strongest thing:** **§04, الريال الذي لم يصل.** A payment fails, the rail turns amber, and the dot *reverses direction and travels home to the child's card by itself* while you read. Twelve seconds, no support ticket, no human. That is the finest individual section in all six documents — it takes the most boring bullet on the comparison table (automatic refund) and makes it the most emotionally persuasive thing on the page. "The scrollbar is the money" is the second-best craft idea in the set.

**Fatal flaw:** It is Concept 3 in an accountant's costume, and it inherits Concept 3's error — it organises the entire site around one riyal when the customer's anxiety is denominated in 97 machines. It then adds the tells of a mood board on top: gold leaf used exactly once, Newsreader italic reserved for English pull-quotes, "a central-bank annual report shot by a cinematographer." Those are wardrobe decisions dressed as strategy. And the two-ground Vault/Statement system means committing to two complete art directions, doubling the surface area where this can go wrong, for a conceptual payoff of one hard cut.

## SECRETLY THE SAME CONCEPT

**There are not six concepts here. There are four, and arguably three.**

**COMMAND (1) and NETWORK (2) are the same concept.** Identical dark instrument ground, identical rationing of cyan to live-state, identical mono/tabular telemetry, identical hairline-and-tick texture vocabulary, identical persistent status rail, identical drag-to-breach. The only difference is the shape of the hero primitive: a horizon line with 97 ticks versus a country outline with 97 nodes. NETWORK is COMMAND with a worse chassis bolted on, because the chassis it chose (a national map) contradicts the claim it needs to make. Do not present these as alternatives to the client — they are one route.

**TAP (3) and LEDGER (6) are the same concept.** Both are a single continuous line, running the full length of the page, carrying one transaction from a fingertip to the operator's bank account, past timestamped stations, with the middleman drawn and then struck out, the auto-refund shown as the line healing or reversing, and the line resolving into the CTA. TAP is the film version; LEDGER is the receipt version. LEDGER dresses it in an annual-report costume and adds a better failure section; that is a wardrobe change, not a second idea.

**And a structural note that outranks all of the above:** **all six concepts have the identical signature moment** — drag the machine outside the geofence ring, watch the page change colour, watch the device die, release, watch it recover, read the Arabic shutdown log. Six independent submissions converged on the same 120 lines of JavaScript. That is strong evidence it is the right interaction, and it is proof that it is **not a differentiator between these pitches.** Whoever builds it must understand that shipping the drag-to-breach is not shipping a concept — it is shipping the table stakes that every one of these six already assumed. The concept is what the drag is *embedded in*.

---

## GREENLIGHT: **CONCEPT 1 — COMMAND / THE BRIDGE**

Because it is the only submission where the metaphor is not a metaphor. R.Pay does not "feel like" a control room — R.Pay *sells* a control room, and the site being one collapses the distance between the pitch and the product to zero. Every other concept requires the buyer to accept an analogy first: that his business is a riyal, or a chain, or an object on a plinth, or a specimen sheet. COMMAND asks him to accept nothing. It also makes the category distinction — *this is fleet command, not a payment gateway* — inside three seconds and before a word is read, which is the single most valuable thing the homepage can do, because every competitor's site currently mis-sells R.Pay's advantage by looking like a payment gateway.

It is also the least mood-board of the six by a wide margin. It ships with laws, not references: cyan means live; instruments settle, data ticks linear; exactly one spring on the entire site; no element animates from opacity 0; nothing decorative moves. Those constrain a real engineer on a real Tuesday. "A central-bank annual report shot by a cinematographer" does not.

And it is the only one that answers the owner's actual complaint correctly. He said wow must not mean more glow. COMMAND's wow is that the website is visibly built by the people who could also build the dashboard — competence made demonstrable rather than claimed. That is the strongest available proof point for a company selling operational command, and it is the one thing a competitor cannot copy in a week, because copying it requires having the geofence.

**The condition on the greenlight is non-negotiable, and the concept names it itself:** if R.Pay will not commit to real data — timestamped, sourced, and wired to a live aggregate endpoint in phase two — do not build this. A payments company caught performing competence it does not have is worse off than one that shipped a nice gradient.

**And one mandatory correction before build:** COMMAND as written is too close to the existing palette to read as a leap. The brass hairline system is the escape hatch and it is currently under-committed — brass must be pushed until it is the first thing a stranger notices, because a warm structural line in a category that is uniformly cold is the cheapest genuine differentiation available and it costs nothing at runtime.

## TRANSPLANTS INTO THE WINNER

**From NETWORK (2) — take two things, both high value:**
- **The export card.** `احفظ اللقطة` → renders the live breach console state into a 1080×1350 canvas → `navigator.share`. This market closes on WhatsApp forwards between operators; NETWORK is the only concept that built for the forward instead of hoping for it. Highest-ROI transplant in the entire set.
- **The sensor-feed frame.** COMMAND discards the existing AI films entirely — correct instinct, wrong conclusion. Put them in a 320×180 frame with a timecode and a LIVE dot and cheap footage reads as surveillance. That reclaims four existing assets for free and reinforces the fiction.

**From LEDGER (6):**
- **§04 wholesale.** COMMAND currently renders auto-refund as "a reverse pulse on the same diagram" — the weakest beat in the winning concept. Replace it with LEDGER's failed riyal that turns around and goes home by itself, unprompted, while you watch. Give it its own section and its own silence.
- **The new Saudi Riyal glyph (﷼) as inline SVG at every settled figure.** Small, correct, and unmistakably of-this-moment in this market.

**From RESTRAINT (4) — the most important transplant, and it is structural:**
- **Invert the inversion.** RESTRAINT earns one dark section on a white page. COMMAND should earn **one daylight section on a dark page** — a clean, warm, properly typeset document for the venue-side audience (Roshn, Kinan, Hamat, the mall operator's procurement lead) who is not a machine operator and will find eleven sections of instrumentation cold. This simultaneously fixes COMMAND's two named risks: the second-audience problem, and dark-palette legibility on a phone at 40% brightness in a Riyadh mall.
- **The persistent appended breach log**, including the payoff after the fifth drag: **«لن تملّ من هذا. ولا نحن.»** It rewards replay, it is the only humour in any of the six, and humour from an instrument is disproportionately memorable.
- **كرّاس المواصفات** — a genuinely beautiful printed spec booklet as the tertiary lead magnet. A site that presents itself as an instrument should of course have a manual.

**From TAP (3):**
- **The rule that no CTA appears before station 04.** COMMAND puts a CTA in the hero. Delete it. Earning three sections of trust before asking is a large part of why a page reads as premium rather than as a landing page.
- **The flat `/الأسعار` and `/التقنية` pages outside the narrative.** The film converts believers; flat pages serve auditors. COMMAND has no answer today for the procurement manager who arrives with one question about settlement windows.

**From AXIS (5) — take exactly one thing, and only conditionally:**
- **Name the terminal, and give it one dimensioned orthographic plate** inside COMMAND's §06 الأساطيل. It converts R.Pay from a service company into a company that makes a thing, and it costs a single illustration. **Condition:** every spec on that plate must be true and verifiable. If R.Pay sources the device, name it and show its dimensions — do not claim it was engineered in Riyadh.

**Take nothing else from AXIS.**

########## JUDGE: TECH-FEASIBILITY ##########
**JUDGE: PRINCIPAL FRONTEND ENGINEER — BUILDABILITY / PERF / ASSET COST / A11Y / MAINTAINABILITY**

Repo facts I verified before scoring (they change several claims):
- `public/fonts/` contains **only** Readex Pro 400/600/700 and Plex Mono 400/500. **IBM Plex Sans Arabic is not in the repo** — every concept that specs it (1, 2, 3, 5, 6) is adding ~2 new woff2 files, not reusing. Concept 3's "both families are already in the repo, zero new font cost" is **false**.
- `public/assets/flow/` is **46 MB**, of which 45 MB is `master.mp4.part00/01/02` — three 20 MB chunks of a video nothing serves. There is a fourth 20 MB `master.mp4.part00` at repo root. This is currently in the Vercel deploy. **Only Concept 4 names it.**
- `flow/seq` is 72 WebP = 1.4 MB (~19 kB/frame) — genuinely cheap, real, reusable.
- Logos: 12 raster `.webp` + 1 `.svg` (roshn). Any "single-ink" treatment (concepts 1, 4, 5, 6) means **re-tracing 12 raster logos to SVG** — a real 2–3 day design task, not a CSS filter.
- `three@0.149.0` + `@types/three` present. All six delete it. Correct, unanimous, uninteresting.
- `playwright` is already a devDependency with a `shoot` script — visual-regression CI is nearly free on any of these.

## CONCEPT 1 — COMMAND (The Bridge) — **9/10**

**Strongest thing:** it is the only concept where the wow mechanism and the cheap mechanism are the same mechanism. The signature moment is `pointermove → write one CSS custom property → CSS does everything downstream`, and the breach is one class on `<html>` swapping tokens. ~4 kB of JS for the highest-impact interaction in the set. It specs a full keyboard path (focusable chip, arrow keys at 8% increments, Escape recentre, breach fires identically) — **the only concept besides 5 that does**, and 5's is a fallback slider rather than an equivalent path. Zero new imagery required: it builds entirely from what is on disk today plus one 3 kB grain tile. Hero is server HTML with an inline 1.2 kB script, so LCP is text.

**Near-fatal flaw:** the global breach — "every token that resolves to `--c-signal` transitions to graphite over 400ms, simultaneously" — is a style-recalc + paint across the entire document on a device with no headroom. It is the one place the concept's own performance thesis breaks, and it happens at the exact moment the visitor is judging the product's competence. Fixable (drive it through `currentColor` inheritance on a bounded set of nodes, or accept an instant swap on `deviceMemory ≤ 4`), but it must be prototyped on a real Galaxy A-series in week one, not week five. Secondary: dark-only, no light mode, admitted-in-brief legibility problem on a mid-tier panel at 40% brightness in a mall — and the proposed "high contrast toggle" is a second full theme someone maintains forever.

## CONCEPT 2 — NETWORK (Silent Grid) — **6/10**

**Strongest thing:** "the hero is fully alive with zero JavaScript." Server-rendered inline SVG, nodes as `<circle>` with staggered CSS keyframes, LCP is an SVG paint. That is the correct instinct and the highest-integrity performance claim in the set.

**Near-fatal flaw:** it does not survive its own component count. **97 SVG `<circle>` elements pulsing on independent CSS keyframe delays inside one `<svg>` do not get individual compositor layers** — SVG children promote unreliably, so you get a full repaint of the map region every frame, on the LCP element, on a 4-core Android. Then it stacks a Canvas 2D arc layer, a 24h scrub dial, a live fleet estimator that mutates the map, a white-label restyle, and — worst — a **1080×1350 canvas share-card export containing Arabic text**. Arabic in `canvas.fillText` gives you shaping but no reliable bidi line-layout, no letter-form QA, and no way to catch a broken join except by eyeballing every render. That single feature is a week of misery and will ship visibly wrong. Scope is roughly double the stated 4–5 weeks.

---

## CONCEPT 3 — TAP (Unbroken) — **5/10**

**Strongest thing:** the best idea in the entire set, strategically — information architecture *is* product architecture, and a features grid genuinely cannot express causality. Also the only concept that correctly identifies scrubbed `<video>` `currentTime` as the mid-tier Android disaster it is and refuses it.

**Fatal flaw:** the unbroken line. A single continuous SVG stroke threading through thirteen stations, across every breakpoint, mirrored between RTL and LTR, with `stroke-dashoffset` scroll-bound, is the most fragile artefact proposed by any of the six. Its geometry is a function of the final laid-out document — so **any copy edit, any longer Arabic paragraph, any new section, any font fallback swap re-authors the path**. You cannot hand this to a client to maintain, and you cannot let a marketing hire touch a heading. The moment you make it survivable by splitting it into per-station segments, it stops being unbroken and the concept's entire premise evaporates. Add a mandatory 2-day plate shoot (hand, terminal macro, chute, desk), ~90 new frames at 2.2 MB, and a permanent dual code path (`animation-timeline: view()` + rAF fallback), and this is the highest-maintenance concept that is also the most brittle.

## CONCEPT 4 — RESTRAINT (Daylight) — **9/10**

**Strongest thing:** it is the only concept where the premium look and the performance budget are the *same decision*, and it proves it with a testable acceptance criterion: *"if you remove all motion from this site, it is still 95% as good."* That sentence is a reduced-motion spec, an a11y spec, and a perf budget in one line — I would adopt it as the build gate regardless of which concept wins. I take that at face value: the stated fallback to IBM Plex Sans Arabic is a *text* face with no display authority at 11vw, and on a page that is 82% empty paper with nothing but type, a merely-adequate headline face is not a degradation, it is a failure. ## CONCEPT 5 — OBJECT (Axis) — **4/10**

**Strongest thing:** the LCP pattern is the best-specified in the set — frame 001 as a plain `<img fetchpriority="high">`, canvas takes over silently at hydration. ## CONCEPT 6 — LEDGER (One Riyal) — **7/10**

**Strongest thing:** the rail is one component doing two jobs — it is the narrative device *and* the scroll-progress indicator — and it is compositor-cheap: one precomputed 200-point `getPointAtLength` LUT, one `translate3d`, one shared rAF, with `animation-timeline: scroll()` on the majority Android path and rAF only as the iOS fallback. ## GREENLIGHT: **CONCEPT 1 — COMMAND**

Not because it out-designs Daylight. It doesn't, quite. ## SECRETLY THE SAME CONCEPT

There are **three** concepts here, not six.

- **1 COMMAND ≡ 2 NETWORK.** Identical: dark instrument aesthetic, persistent live status rail, mono timestamped log lines, drag-a-node-out-of-a-geofence signature, comparison-as-signal-grid, white-label toy, live counters, "delete three," "cyan means data not decoration." The *only* material difference is that 2 draws a map of Saudi Arabia where 1 draws a row of 97 ticks — and 2 pays for that map with a heavier LCP element, a data artefact to maintain, and four extra client components. **Concept 2 is Concept 1 with worse engineering.** It has no independent claim on the shortlist.
- **3 UNBROKEN ≡ 6 ONE RIYAL.** Identical: one continuous line/rail carrying a single unit (a tap / a riyal) from machine to bank across the whole page, with numbered stations, mono timestamps, a middleman fork that gets erased, an auto-refund reversal beat, and a geofence interruption partway down. **Concept 6 is Concept 3 built from assets that exist; Concept 3 is Concept 6 that requires a photo shoot and an unmaintainable path.** Same idea, one buildable version.
- **4 DAYLIGHT is the only genuinely orthogonal proposal in the set** — it is the only one that isn't dark, isn't instrument-cosplay, and doesn't buy its wow with bytes.

And the thing nobody in the room seems to have noticed: **all six concepts have the same signature moment.** Drag the machine out of the circle, watch it die, the page changes colour, it heals on release. Six independent concepts converged on it, which is strong evidence it's correct — but it means the signature moment **is not a differentiator between these concepts.** Only its price differs: Concept 4 buys it for 120 lines and no images; Concept 5 buys it for a 3D pipeline, WebAudio and a tilt sensor. Judge accordingly.
